Siddhesh tends to write in a style that combines:

He likes ideas that sound:
·	intelligent without becoming academic
·	philosophical without becoming pretentious
·	provocative without becoming needlessly offensive
·	casual but meaningful
·	memorable
·	quotable
·	occasionally cinematic
·	occasionally absurd
He particularly likes writing that makes someone stop and think:
“Wait. That's actually an interesting way of looking at it.”
Do not flatten everything into generic motivational quotes.
Do not turn every thought into “Believe in yourself!” nonsense.
When critiquing an idea:
1.	Explain what is genuinely strong.
2.	Identify what is logically weak.
3.	Identify hidden assumptions.
4.	Distinguish rhetoric from factual claims.
5.	Preserve the original character of the thought.
6.	Suggest refinements only when they materially improve it.

3.43 Money, value and creating wealth
This was one of Siddhesh's longer essays.
Original version:
"When people say "You can't make money without money", it's mostly because they confuse money with something that is some magical wand, when in reality Money is a physical embodiment of 'Value'. In reality you need Value that is in demand to make money. When you give money to someone, it means you're giving them a 'Value Coupon' which they can exchange it with any other object, service etc that they feel is of value to them. So if you Focus on Value more than its physical embodiment i.e. money, you'll start realising that you need value more than money in order to make money."
Later version:
"When people say "You can't make money without money", it's mostly because they confuse money with something that is some magical wand, when in reality Money is like a value claim ticket of 'Value'. In reality you need Value that is in demand to make money. When you give money to someone, it means you're giving them a 'Value Coupon' which they can exchange it with any other object, service etc that they feel is of value to them. So if you Focus on Value more than its ticket i.e. money, you'll start realising that you need value more than money in order to make money."
The key conceptual refinement discussed:
Money is better thought of as:

Instead of obsessing over money, focus on creating something people value and demand.
Important nuance:
The claim:
“You can't make money without money”

Money is one form of capital, but other forms of capital include:
·	skills
·	knowledge
·	relationships
·	reputation
·	distribution
·	labour
·	creativity
